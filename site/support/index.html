Set up Sphere and start reviewing

Follow the steps for your situation. Installing Sphere, getting course approval and connecting your browser are separate parts of setup.

Choose where to start

  • First time using Sphere? Start with what you need, then follow installation, access request and confirmation in order.
  • Already approved for this course? Go to returning reviewers. Do not create another account just because you updated or changed computers.
  • Used an old Sphere or RADCRA pilot? Follow the old-copy replacement steps so you do not run both extensions.
  • Something is not working? Find your symptom under troubleshooting.

Before you start

  • Use Google Chrome on your computer, in the profile you normally use for reviewing courses. A Chrome profile is the separate browser workspace selected through the profile picture at the top right.
  • Have the course link from your course team and permission to open that course in UCO Moodle or LEARN. Your normal university/Moodle login is separate from Sphere.
  • Use an email inbox you can access. Use the same email address each time you sign in to Sphere.
  • The course team must have enabled Sphere for your course. If they have not, installing the extension alone will not enable it.
Two kinds of access

Moodle access lets you open the course. Sphere approval lets you review it with a particular role. Neither replaces the other.

1. Install and open Sphere

  1. Open the official listing in Chrome.

    Use the link below in your usual course-reviewing Chrome profile. Sphere is unlisted, so use this link rather than searching for a similarly named extension.

    Open Sphere in the Chrome Web Store

  2. Choose Add to Chrome.

    Read the permission prompt and choose Add extension if you agree. If the Store copy is already installed, do not add an unpacked ZIP as well.

    What you should see: Sphere appears in Chrome’s Extensions menu. A work-managed browser may require your IT team to allow installation.

  3. Optionally pin Sphere to the toolbar.

    Open Chrome’s puzzle-piece Extensions menu and use the pin beside Sphere. This makes its controls easier to find; pinning is not the same as signing in.

  4. Open your course and refresh the page.

    Sign in to UCO Moodle or LEARN using your usual course login. If the course was open during installation, refresh it. Read Sphere’s first-use notice and select Continue only if you understand and accept the described data use.

    What you should see: a Sphere strip or panel at the bottom right. For a first-time reviewer it normally says Signed out. The chevron expands a compact strip.

If you cannot see the panel, use the missing-panel checklist before creating any accounts.

2. Request access to your course

Use these steps if your course uses Sphere’s email approval flow and you have not yet been approved for that course.

  1. In Sphere, choose Sign in, then Request access.

    Use the panel on the course you actually need to review. Approval is course-specific.

  2. Enter your name, email address and requested role.

    Choose the role agreed with your course team, such as SME for a subject matter expert. No Sphere password is needed. Your administrator assigns the actual role; selecting a requested role does not grant it.

  3. Choose Request access to submit.

    Sphere sends your request to the administrator’s pending list and queues an admin notification email.

    What you should see: a message explaining approval and email confirmation. This is not yet a Connected state, and it does not mean your request has been approved.

  4. Wait for the administrator to approve it.

    The administrator reviews the course and requested role. Human approval is required. You will receive an approval email when access is granted.

    What happens next: look for a Course access approved email from Sphere Notifications. It states the assigned course role.

Not seeing this form?

Some older UCO courses use an invitation or another approved sign-in route. If Sphere asks for a code, or says the course is not enabled, ask your course team which route to use. Do not create a duplicate account.

3. Confirm your email and connect

  1. Check the approval email.

    Check the course title and assigned role. Access emails are sent by a scheduled worker and can take a few minutes; check spam as well as your inbox. Waiting for admin approval is separate from that delivery delay.

  2. Open the confirmation link in the same Chrome profile.

    Use the profile where you submitted the request, not another browser or an Incognito window. Follow the email’s instruction to confirm within 20 minutes.

    If your email app opens another browser, copy the confirmation link and paste it into the address bar of the correct Chrome profile. Do not forward that private link or include it in a support screenshot.

  3. On the Sphere page, choose Confirm email.

    This proves you can access the approved email address.

    What you should see: an Email verified message and a link back to the course. Verification alone has not yet connected the extension.

  4. Return to Moodle and choose Check access.

    Open the course link, then use Sign in → Check access in the Sphere panel. A short browser authentication window may open and close.

    Setup is complete when: Sphere shows Connected, your reviewer name and the assigned course role. Expand the panel if those details are hidden in its compact view.

If the link expired or opened in the wrong profile, follow the email-link recovery steps; do not delete your account.

Already approved, or using another computer?

Your usual Chrome profile

Open the course and refresh it if needed. Sphere normally remembers approved course access. If it says Connected, you do not need to request access again.

Signed out, a new computer or a different profile

  1. Open the approved course and choose Sign in.

    Use the ordinary reviewer sign-in, not Admin sign-in. Enter the email address already approved for that course and select Sign in.

  2. Send and follow the email sign-in link.

    If a new Sphere sign-in tab opens, check your email address and choose Send sign-in link. Opening that tab does not send the email automatically. A new computer or browser profile needs email confirmation. Open the emailed link in that same profile and choose Confirm email. Use the confirmation steps above.

  3. Return to the course and choose Check access.

    You do not need a new account or a new role request for a course where you are already approved.

    What you should see: Connected with the existing course role and the comments that role is allowed to see.

Signing out, clearing browser data or using another profile may require a new email link. Course approval does not automatically approve you for a different course. If your older course uses a different access route, contact the course team.

Administrator mode is different

Admin sign-in uses the administrator account and activates all-course administrator mode. It is not needed for an SME. To return to reviewer mode, sign out of Sphere and use ordinary Sign in with the reviewer’s approved email; a fresh link may be needed.

Replace an old Sphere or RADCRA pilot

Chrome treats the manually loaded pilot and the Store copy as different extensions. Installing the Store copy does not automatically remove the pilot.

  1. Save any unfinished comment first.

    Saved comments and course approvals live on the server. An unsaved draft or an old extension’s local preferences may not survive disabling or removal.

  2. Install the official Store copy.

    Use the Sphere Store listing. Do not download another old ZIP.

  3. Disable the old copy, without removing it yet.

    Open Chrome’s puzzle-piece menu and choose Manage Extensions, or use Chrome’s menu → Extensions → Manage Extensions. Find the manually loaded Sphere/RADCRA card and switch it off. Keep the Store copy on. An Unpacked extension badge can help identify the old pilot; do not rely on version number alone.

  4. Connect the Store copy and check the expected comments.

    Refresh the course and sign in using your existing approved email. Your old extension’s browser sign-in may not transfer automatically.

    Before removal: the Store copy says Connected, shows the correct role and loads the expected course feedback. Only one Sphere copy should be running.

  5. Now remove the old copy.

    Back in Manage Extensions, choose Remove on the old manually loaded Sphere/RADCRA card and confirm Chrome’s removal prompt. Leave the Store copy installed.

    What stays: server-held comments, saved review history and approved course roles. Removing an extension does not delete your Sphere account. The old copy’s local state is removed.

There is no automatic old-copy remover in the current release. If you cannot identify which card is the old pilot, ask for help before removing anything.

What your course role means

The administrator chooses the role for each course. Two reviewers may legitimately see different comment lists.

Beta tester
Sees their own comment threads and the replies available to them.
SME (subject matter expert)
Sees their own comments, SME-authored comments and threads explicitly shared with them. This does not grant access to every reviewer’s private feedback.
Learning Designer / DCD
Manages course feedback, including comment status and sharing feedback with an SME.
Administrator
Approves access and assigned roles. Admin sign-in is a separate all-course mode, not a role new reviewers can grant themselves.

Copying a Moodle course to LEARN does not automatically copy its Sphere comments or reviewer approvals. Ask the course team how review access is being arranged for the copied course.

For course teams: invite your reviewers

  1. Arrange course registration first.

    Send the exact course link privately to your Sphere administrator or authorised course lead. UCO Moodle and LEARN are separate course locations, even if the course titles match.

  2. Confirm the access route.

    A registered LEARN course needs new email access requests explicitly enabled in its administration settings. Registration alone does not turn them on. Existing UCO pilots may use a different route.

  3. Send each reviewer the setup guide and course link.

    Tell them which role to request. They still need access to the underlying Moodle/LEARN course. Do not share your administrator login.

  4. Review Pending requests.

    The administrator receives an access-request email and sees pending requests at the top of the admin page. Check the person and course, select their actual course role and approve. Sphere then emails the reviewer to confirm and connect.

Disabling new enrollment does not revoke an already-approved reviewer. Contact your administrator if existing access needs to be revoked. Ask which comment-notification features are enabled for your course; access-approval emails do not imply every type of comment email is enabled.

Troubleshooting

Choose the symptom below. Avoid repeated requests or duplicate accounts while troubleshooting.

I cannot see the Sphere panel
  1. Check the correct Chrome profile. The extension may be installed in another profile.
  2. Open Manage Extensions and check that the Store copy of Sphere is enabled.
  3. In Sphere’s Details, check site access for the relevant UCO Moodle or LEARN site. Review any new permission request after an update.
  4. Open Sphere’s toolbar controls and check whether review has been paused. Resume it if needed.
  5. Refresh the course and complete the first-use notice if it appears. Look for a compact strip at the bottom right and expand its chevron.
  6. If two old/new copies are present, follow the pilot replacement steps.
I requested access but no email arrived

New access requests need human approval first. The initial request notifies the administrator; it is not an immediate approval email to you. After approval, allow a few minutes for scheduled delivery and check spam for Sphere Notifications. Check the spelling of the email you submitted. If necessary, contact your course administrator instead of creating another account.

My email link expired or says “different Chrome profile”

For an already-approved course, return to Sphere’s ordinary Sign in, enter the approved email and request a fresh sign-in link. In the new Sphere sign-in tab, check the email address and choose Send sign-in link; simply opening the tab does not send an email. Open the emailed link in the same Chrome profile and confirm promptly, then return to the course and choose Check access. If the email app opens Safari, Brave or another Chrome profile, copy the link into the correct profile instead. Do not forward the link to another person.

Email verified, but Sphere still says Signed out

Email verification and connecting the extension are separate. Return to the original course and use Sign in → Check access in the same Chrome profile. If another browser handled the confirmation, follow the email-link recovery steps above.

The course is not enabled, or Sphere asks for an invitation code

The course may not be registered, may not accept new email requests, or may use an older invitation route. Ask your course team to check the exact course link and access method. Installing Sphere or submitting a different email does not enable the course.

My name, role or comments do not look right

Check the signed-in email and course. An existing account can retain its earlier display name. Ask your administrator to correct the account name or course role if needed. Admin sign-in activates administrator mode; use normal reviewer Sign in for SME access.

For missing comments, check Whole course versus This page, and Open versus Resolved. Your role controls which threads you can see. A copied LEARN course is not the same Sphere workspace as its original UCO course.

I see two panels, or an older version keeps appearing

Check for an unpacked Sphere or RADCRA copy as well as the Store copy. Save unfinished work, disable the old copy and refresh. Only remove it after the Store copy connects and loads the expected comments. Follow the full replacement steps.

My work computer will not allow installation

Your organisation may manage Chrome extensions. Ask your IT team whether the official Sphere Store item can be allowed. Do not bypass the organisation’s security controls. Google also provides official installation and extension-management help.

Privacy and help

For access or account questions, contact your course administrator first. For Sphere support, email rcadavies@gmail.com.

Describe the step that failed, the visible error and whether you use a Store or unpacked copy. Never send passwords, confirmation links, tokens, invitation codes or real student data. Remove private course content and browser/account details from screenshots. Course links should be shared privately with your authorised course team, not posted in a public issue.

What data does Sphere handle?

Sphere handles reviewer name, email, role, and approval status; authentication, session, reconnect, and device credentials; course/page URLs and activity/SCORM identifiers, titles, and current location; comments, replies, statuses, timestamps, selected text, visual anchors, and optional screenshots and attachments submitted only at the reviewer's request; and browser/build, navigation, reliability, and error diagnostics. Authorised course-team members may read course-review content. Service operator staff do not routinely read it and may access specific data only on explicit user request about that data, for security or abuse, or legal obligation. Internal operational or improvement use is limited to aggregated and anonymised data.

GitHub Pages receives ordinary request metadata: IP address, user agent, request time, and requested path. Sphere sends it no Moodle content, review content, credentials, or tokens.
