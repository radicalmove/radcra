Get started

  1. In the Chrome profile you use for Moodle, install Sphere from the Chrome Web Store. Skip this if the Store copy is already installed.
  2. Open the course link from your course team and sign in to Moodle. Refresh if the page was open before installation. Read Sphere’s first-run notice and choose Continue if you agree.
  3. If you haven’t been approved for that course and it uses email approval, choose Sign in → Request access. Enter your name, email and requested role. No Sphere password is needed. Wait for admin approval, then open the approval email and confirm your email in the same Chrome profile within 20 minutes. Your administrator assigns the actual course role.
  4. Return to the course, choose Sign in → Check access and look for Connected. If the course isn’t enabled or email access isn’t available, ask your course team for help. Installing the extension does not grant Moodle or Sphere course access.

If an approved email link expires, use Sign in with your approved email address to request a fresh link. Do not create another account. Access emails are sent by a scheduled worker and can take a few minutes; check spam as well as your inbox.

If the Sphere panel is missing

In Chrome, open the puzzle-piece menu and choose Manage Extensions. Check that Sphere is installed and enabled, and that site access permits the Moodle or LEARN site you are reviewing. Review any new permission request after an update, then refresh the course page. If an old unpacked Sphere is installed, disable that copy once the Store version is working.

Already approved or switching versions?

Reviewers should use ordinary Sign in with their approved email address. Admin sign-in is for administrators and switches Sphere into all-course administrator mode; it is not needed to connect an SME.

Use your usual Chrome profile and the email address you were approved with. Chrome updates Store installs automatically. If Chrome disables Sphere after an update, review the new permission and choose Accept permissions if you agree, then refresh Moodle.

If you used an unpacked Sphere or RADCRA pilot, install the Store copy and disable the old copy under chrome://extensions. Your existing course approval and comments stay in Sphere, but you may need to reconnect. Once the Store copy shows Connected and your expected course comments are available, remove the old copy using its Remove button in Manage Extensions. Removing the old extension does not delete server-held comments or course approvals. Where email sign-in is available, use the approved address and open any confirmation link in the same Chrome profile. Otherwise, ask your course team to help you reconnect rather than creating another account.

Request review on another course

Send the course link and intended reviewer role privately to your course team. An authorised administrator or learning designer registers the course, and access is approved separately. Registering a course does not automatically turn on email onboarding.

Privacy and support

Sphere handles reviewer name, email, role, and approval status; authentication, session, reconnect, and device credentials; course/page URLs and activity/SCORM identifiers, titles, and current location; comments, replies, statuses, timestamps, selected text, visual anchors, and optional screenshots and attachments submitted only at the reviewer's request; and browser/build, navigation, reliability, and error diagnostics. Authorised course-team members may read course-review content. Service operator staff do not routinely read it and may access specific data only on explicit user request about that data, for security or abuse, or legal obligation. Internal operational or improvement use is limited to aggregated and anonymised data.

GitHub Pages receives ordinary request metadata: IP address, user agent, request time, and requested path. Sphere sends it no Moodle content, review content, credentials, or tokens.

Read the privacy policy. Contact rcadavies@gmail.com or use GitHub issues.

Keep reports safe

Never email or post credentials, tokens, invitation codes, private course links, real student data, or unnecessary course content.
